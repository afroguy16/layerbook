# Mechanic Booking App: Competitor & Market Scan (Oct 2026)

Supporting evidence for the Idea brief: Auto Book. Written 15 Oct 2026 for an earlier framing (customer-paid N500 booking); the brief has the current decisions.

Compiled 15 Oct 2026 (WAT). Desk research only; nobody was contacted. Links were checked with WebFetch or curl. "Status" means what the source shows on the date given. "?" marks something uncertain.

## 1. Similar products

**Nigeria**

- **Fixit45** (Lagos, founded 2021). Book repairs and diagnostics online, with pickup and delivery, through 100+ partner garages. Also sells parts (Xparts) and does CNG conversion. **Active**: the site is live with ©2026 and claims 50,000+ repairs. It raised a $1.9M pre-seed in Sep 2023 and said it then had 300+ workshops in 9 cities and \~4,000 clients. <https://fixit45.com/> · <https://disruptafrica.com/2023/09/25/nigerian-auto-tech-startup-fixit45-raises-1-9m-pre-seed-funding-round/>
- **Mecho Autotech** (founded 2021). Connects individuals and fleets to 110+ approved workshops. **Pivoted/expanded** into wholesale spare parts and finance, raising $2.4M pre-Series A in Sep 2023. <https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/14/mecho-autotech-raises-2-4m-ventures-into-wholesale-spare-parts-distribution/>
- **Carviva** (founded 2020). App to book workshops near you; you pay cash or card at the workshop. Site claims "100+ service centres, 20 Lagos zones, 1000+ downloads". **Site is live**, but how active the business is remains uncertain (LinkedIn shows only 2 staff, and it has broadened into fuel, parts and BNPL). <https://www.mycarviva.com/>
- **SureMech**. Mobile mechanics booked by WhatsApp, web or phone, with digital job records. **Active?** Its last verified post is from May 2025. <https://suremech.com/contents/57566556>
- **Autochek**. Car financing marketplace that also offers maintenance through 150+ partner service centres. **Active.** <https://www.startuplist.africa/startups/autochek>
- **AutoSave** (Lagos, 2014–2017). Roadside mechanic and towing marketplace. **Shut down in mid-2017.** <https://techpoint.africa/feature/why-autosave-shut-down/>
- **Autobox** (Lagos). Online parts and car repair. Backed by Lagos Angel Network and CcHub. **Faded away**: last tweet May 2016, site down by Jul 2017. <https://techpoint.africa/general/nigerian-startups-fail/>

**Kenya**

- **UrDrive** (Nairobi, 2021). On-demand mechanics. Bootstrapped after a KSh600k grant, with 700+ cars serviced as of Apr 2024. **Active?** Tracxn lists it as unfunded with 5 staff (Aug 2026). <https://nation.africa/kenya/business/enterprise/on-demand-mechanic-app-revolutionises-car-repair-industry-4592374> · [https://tracxn.com/d/companies/urdrive/\_\_BcuX4WIrfNVsxJr27PjtVaPOGijuefBjCIKh-pDsvQE](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/urdrive/__BcuX4WIrfNVsxJr27PjtVaPOGijuefBjCIKh-pDsvQE)
- **Gialfyi Car Clinic** (Nairobi, live since Jul 2022). Website to find a mechanic and book. Charges car owners a small fee; mechanics are listed free. Status uncertain (the Apr 2024 profile is the latest found). <https://biasharaleo.co.ke/gialfyi-car-clinic-facilitating-car-servicing-at-the-touch-of-a-button/>
- **MyMech, Mekpap, RepairNet** (Nairobi garage aggregators, founded 2019–2020). Tracxn marks them **"Deadpooled"** (same Tracxn link). No reasons were found.
- **Sokompare**. Vehicle marketplace that **pivoted** to the general directory Findit in 2019. <https://disruptafrica.com/2019/06/06/kenyan-startup-sokompare-pivots-to-service-provider-marketplace-model/>

**Ghana / South Africa**

- **Mechanico** (HQ Estonia; offices in Accra and Johannesburg). Get quotes from workshops and book. Site is **live** and lists "32 workshops, 4 markets", which is small. <https://mechanico.io/>
- **ServiceMyCar** (SA, launched 2020). Quotes from several workshops plus booking. Raised ZAR5M seed. Site **live** (HTTP 200, Oct 2026). <https://disruptafrica.com/2020/11/13/sa-auto-tech-startup-launches-with-311k-seed-funding/>
- **Michanic** (SA, 2016). Instant quotes from vetted mechanics on a revenue share. Had 30,000+ quotes by Nov 2021. Site **live** (HTTP 200). <https://disruptafrica.com/2021/11/05/sas-michanic-helps-you-bring-qualified-mechanics-to-your-door/>
- I found no Ghana-only mechanic booking startup with a verifiable source.

## 2. Why the stalled ones stalled

- **AutoSave**: "impossible retaining customers… no one wished their cars would keep breaking down." It had no funding and bootstrapped to the end. It could not afford marketing to reach car owners. The Lagos State government then offered a similar service free or discounted. "Mechanics disappointing customers we assigned them to." It was profitable at a 10–15% cut, but only on low volume. (Techpoint, Dec 2019, link above.)
- **Autobox**: no reason was ever made public (Techpoint, Jul 2017).
- **Gialfyi**: people thought it was a scam ("think we are a scam"), some mechanics asked for handouts, and "good marketing is really expensive."
- **Michanic**: earning "just enough income to keep the lights on" (2021).
- **Mecho**: most problems in delivering jobs came from getting quality spare parts. Parts are about 80% of the market's value, against about 20% for service charges, so it moved into parts.
- **Kenyan aggregators (MyMech, Mekpap, RepairNet)**: shown as dead on Tracxn, but **no public reason found**.

## 3. How Lagos mechanics take bookings and keep records today

- Customers find mechanics mostly through referrals and word of mouth (Future Africa on Mecho: <https://www.future.africa/news-insights/mecho-autotech-is-building-the-future-of-automotive-services> ; Gialfyi article, Kenya).
- Common pains on the owner side are "no bookings, no accountability", quotes that jump (₦7,000 becoming ₦23,000) and mechanics who say "I dey come". This is a competitor's marketing copy, so treat it as biased. SureMech itself takes bookings by WhatsApp and phone (link above).
- Records: "Most still use an exercise book" (TrustAm, a WhatsApp bookkeeping tool for mechanics, also vendor copy): <https://www.trustamai.com/business/for/mechanic> . An IGC study of Lagos small firms (not mechanics specifically) found paper records dominate. Adoption of a record-keeping app rose \~95% only when firms got hands-on WhatsApp "micro-consulting": <https://www.theigc.org/sites/default/files/2025-03/Oyebanjo%20Policy%20Brief%20January%202024.pdf>
- Roadside mechanics win on price over formal workshops, which are seen as "for the rich" (FUT Minna survey of 40 mechanics and 16 owners, 2012): <http://irepo.futminna.edu.ng:8080/jspui/bitstream/123456789/23898/1/THE%20CHALLENGES%20OF%20MODERN%20AUTOMOBILE%20WORKSHOP%20AND%20THE%20TRAINING%20STRATEGIES%20FOR%20ROADSIDE%20MECHANICS%20IN%20LAGOS%20STATE.pdf> (link not re-verified)
- No source was found that directly surveys how Lagos mechanics take bookings (WhatsApp vs phone vs walk-in). **This is a gap; do field interviews.**

## 4. Evidence people pay

- **Revenue models**: AutoSave took a 10–15% commission. Mecho earns commissions on repairs plus maintenance subscriptions. UrDrive charges mechanics a referral fee per job; its average job is about KSh1,500. Michanic uses a revenue share. Gialfyi charges car owners "a small fee". **None of the sources found shows a flat fee charged to the customer per booking.**
- **Traction**: Fixit45 had \~4,000 clients in 2023 and claims 50k+ repairs on its site (unverified). Mecho had 6,000+ cars by 2023. Carviva claims 1,000+ downloads. UrDrive had 700+ cars by 2024.
- **Spend and funding**: Nigerian car owners spend about $650 a year on parts and maintenance, and the aftermarket is about $8B (Mecho's figures, via TechCrunch). Mecho raised $2.4M, Fixit45 $1.9M, ServiceMyCar ZAR5M, and UrDrive got a KSh600k grant. Mechanic jobs typically run ₦5,000–₦200,000 (TrustAm, vendor claim).

## Takeaways for the N500 fee model

1. **The fee is small next to a job.** N500 is under 10% of even a ₦5k job, so it shouldn't put owners off. But at that size you need very high volume: 10,000 completed bookings a month makes only ₦5M. Peers make their money from commissions, parts, fleets and finance.
2. **Bypass risk is high.** The owner already knows "their own" mechanic, so after one booking both sides can just WhatsApp each other. The app has to be useful beyond making the booking (history, reminders, receipts) or the fee will be avoided. Consider charging the mechanic, or making the fee part of an in-app payment.
3. **Repeat use is the core problem.** AutoSave died on low repeat use. Planned maintenance reminders and the car's issue history are what bring people back. Lean on them.
4. **Getting mechanics on board is the bottleneck.** Mechanics run on exercise books and referrals. Adoption needed hands-on help (IGC). Expect field onboarding over WhatsApp, not app-store downloads.
5. **Parts are where the money is.** Parts are about 80% of the market, and both funded Nigerian players moved toward parts or B2B fleets. Treat N500 as a test of whether people will pay, not as the business model.
